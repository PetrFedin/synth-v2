## 392. Version 3.5 scope — supplier trust, responsible purchasing and production-network resilience

### 392.1 Why this layer is required

A wholesale platform cannot claim end-to-end control if it knows the brand and the product but cannot prove:

- which legal entity accepted the order;
- which production location is authorized to perform each operation;
- whether the location is real, active and correctly matched;
- whether subcontracting was approved;
- whether the committed capacity is feasible;
- whether buyer changes created avoidable labour, cost or overtime pressure;
- whether workers have access to an effective grievance channel;
- whether identified harm was actually remediated;
- whether audits represent current conditions or only historical snapshots.

Version 3.5 introduces a governed supplier-trust and responsible-purchasing layer between commercial planning and production execution.

```text
Commercial Demand
→ Responsible Purchasing Feasibility
→ Verified Production Network
→ Authorized Production Route
→ Worker / Environmental Due Diligence
→ Capacity and Order Commitment
→ Production Evidence
→ Remediation and Improvement
→ Verified Outcome
```

### 392.2 Evidence boundary

The following benchmarks are used:

- OECD garment and footwear due-diligence guidance for the risk-based due-diligence lifecycle;
- current European Commission CSDDD materials for mandate-profile versioning, not for legal advice;
- Social & Labor Convergence Program CAF for converged social/labour data, verification and controlled data sharing;
- Better Work for the combined assessment, advisory and training improvement model;
- Open Supply Hub for production-location identity, matching, claims and open facility identifiers.

JOOR status remains `NOT CONFIRMED / UAT` unless separately observed. The master map does not state that inaccessible JOOR editions lack these capabilities.

### 394.3 External ID treatment

An external identifier is an interoperability reference, not the sole primary key.

If an external registry merges two facility records:

1. preserve both historical IDs;
2. create an external-identity change event;
3. recalculate affected supplier maps and disclosures;
4. do not merge Syntha facilities automatically if physical evidence conflicts;
5. require stewardship approval for canonical merge;
6. retain every historical order, audit and production event under its original facility context.

## 399. Unauthorized Subcontracting Prevention

### 399.1 Subcontracting lifecycle

```text
SUBCONTRACT_REQUESTED
→ BUSINESS_JUSTIFICATION
→ FACILITY_IDENTITY_RESOLVED
→ CAPABILITY_REVIEW
→ SOCIAL / ENVIRONMENTAL / QUALITY REVIEW
→ COMMERCIAL AND CAPACITY REVIEW
→ APPROVED / CONDITIONAL / REJECTED
→ ROUTE_VERSION_UPDATED
```

### 399.2 Required declaration

- process to be subcontracted;
- quantity and period;
- reason;
- proposed facility;
- relationship to primary supplier;
- worker and capacity implications;
- commercial impact;
- traceability method;
- material and WIP transfer method;
- responsible manager;
- expiry of approval.

### 399.3 Detection signals

- unexpected shipping origin;
- unknown facility in traceability event;
- inconsistent production capacity versus output;
- material movement to an unapproved address;
- invoice or transport document from another entity;
- inspection at a different location;
- unusual geolocation or device pattern;
- repeated output beyond declared line capacity;
- worker grievance referring to an unknown site.

A detection signal opens an investigation. It is not, by itself, proof of misconduct.

### 399.4 Emergency subcontracting

Emergency approval requires:

- documented force-majeure or capacity event;
- limited product/order scope;
- time-bound approval;
- minimum safety and legal checks;
- enhanced inspection;
- customer/brand disclosure where contractually required;
- post-event root-cause review.

### 416.1 Wage record separation

- legal minimum wage;
- collectively bargained wage;
- regular base wage;
- guaranteed compensation;
- overtime;
- bonuses/incentives;
- in-kind benefits;
- deductions;
- take-home pay;
- living-wage benchmark and methodology;
- benchmark date and geography.

### 416.2 Gap formula

```text
Living Wage Gap
= Applicable Living Wage Benchmark
- Eligible Regular Compensation
```

Overtime must not be treated as the default mechanism for closing the gap.

### 416.3 Purchasing-practice linkage

Evaluate whether:

- negotiated price supports lawful and agreed labour cost;
- price reductions assume unrealistic efficiency;
- late changes increase unpaid or excessive overtime risk;
- payment delays affect wage payment;
- short lead time increases labour-broker dependence;
- forecast volatility creates hiring/termination cycles.

### 416.4 Improvement plan

- wage-ladder analysis;
- productivity improvement with worker safeguards;
- price/cost negotiation;
- collective bargaining support where appropriate;
- benefit improvement;
- overtime reduction;
- multi-buyer collaboration;
- outcome monitoring.

### 423.1 Evidence pack

- assurance;
- retention;
- owner/legal review;
- amendment history.

The platform must not hard-code one historical version of a changing law.

### 424.3 Supplier explanation

Risk controls remain mandatory, but historical volume must not become the only path to visibility.

## 429. Version 3.5 UAT scenarios

1. Two facilities have similar names but different coordinates and legal operators; automatic merge is blocked.
2. OS ID merge occurs externally; Syntha preserves aliases and requires stewardship review.
3. Facility owner submits a claim without authority evidence; claim remains pending.
4. Supplier changes payout bank account; independent verification and step-up authentication are required.
5. Ownership change invalidates old trust and agreement assumptions without deleting history.
6. Facility claims sewing capability but trial production fails; passport evidence level is downgraded.
7. Product route contains a suspended dyeing facility; production release is blocked.
8. Supplier requests emergency subcontracting; time-bound scope and enhanced inspection are required.
9. Shipment origin reveals an undeclared facility; investigation opens without automatic guilt conclusion.
10. Capacity plan exceeds lawful working-time assumptions; commitment is marked infeasible.
11. Buyer shortens delivery after confirmation; burden ledger calculates overtime, rework and freight exposure.
12. Supplier proposes delivery extension instead of accepting hidden cost; workflow preserves negotiation.
13. Buyer approval delay moves the critical path; buyer scorecard records responsibility.
14. Order passes price but fails responsible lead-time gate; PO release requires replan or executive exception.
15. Assessment data is imported without a universal score; Syntha applies a separately versioned policy mapping.
16. Existing valid assessment covers most requirements; system requests only missing targeted evidence.
17. Email attachment conflicts with secure gateway reference; secure verified record takes precedence.
18. Critical finding cannot be downgraded by a commercial user.
19. Correction is completed but root cause persists; finding does not close.
20. Buyer purchasing practice contributed to harm; remediation action is assigned to both parties.
21. Anonymous grievance is routed without exposing identity to facility management.
22. Immediate safety grievance activates protected escalation before routine investigation.
23. Worker reports retaliation after complaint; linked protection plan and separate finding are created.
24. Low complaint volume with low awareness is not interpreted as low risk.
25. Recruitment fee case records worker reimbursement and confirmation.
26. Migrant-worker documents are stored under restricted access and excluded from normal supplier analytics.
27. Living-wage assessment excludes overtime from regular compensation by policy.
28. Payment delay correlates with wage-delay signal; causality remains under review rather than asserted automatically.
29. Working-time anomaly is generated from aggregate signals; no automatic worker discipline occurs.
30. Training attendance is complete but outcome indicator does not improve; program remains open.
31. Flood risk affects a unique process facility; continuity scenario identifies exposed products and orders.
32. Alternate factory is technically capable but lacks current labour assessment; transfer remains conditional.
33. Production transfer preserves open remediation obligations at the original facility.
34. Supplier development project verifies measurable quality and worker-safety improvement.
35. Buyer exits a supplier while materials and unpaid invoices remain; responsible wind-down plan is required.
36. Supplier termination does not close an unresolved worker-remediation case.
37. Due-diligence report distinguishes assessment completion from verified remediation outcome.
38. Supplier search displays sponsored placement separately and still enforces eligibility.
39. New supplier with strong verified capability is not hidden solely due to low transaction history.
40. Facility closure event blocks new orders but preserves audits, claims and product history.
41. External facility data contradicts supplier declaration; disagreement is visible and routed to stewardship.
42. Supplier score deteriorates after buyer-caused late changes; balanced scorecard prevents one-sided attribution.
43. Production output exceeds plausible demonstrated capacity; subcontract/capacity investigation opens.
44. A user without worker-voice purpose tries to access grievance evidence; access is denied and audited.

## 431. Version 3.5 delivery roadmap and decisions

### P0 — supplier trust and harm prevention

1. Canonical facility identity and external ID reconciliation.
2. Supplier legal trust, sensitive bank-change controls and continuous re-verification.
3. Production Capability Passport.
4. Authorized Production Route and subcontracting approval.
5. Capacity truth and responsible purchasing gate.
6. Due-diligence program, salient-risk registry and action tracking.
7. Critical finding and remediation model.
8. Protected worker grievance and anti-retaliation controls.
9. Responsible disengagement workflow.

### P1 — convergence and collaborative improvement

1. SLCP/assessment reference integration and duplication check.
2. Buyer change burden and compensation.
3. Responsible lead-time calculator.
4. Balanced buyer-supplier scorecards.
5. Recruitment-chain and fee-remediation workflow.
6. Living-wage and working-time pressure analytics.
7. Worker-management improvement and training programs.
8. Network risk twin and continuity alternatives.
9. Supplier development and co-investment.

### P2 — scalable network intelligence

1. Privacy-safe cross-buyer improvement collaboration.
2. Advanced capability inference with mandatory evidence labels.
3. Network concentration and systemic-risk simulation.
4. Supplier ranking fairness monitoring.
5. Due-diligence evidence-pack automation with legal review.

### Final decisions

1. **Facility identity is a first-class master-data domain.** Supplier name is not enough.
2. **Capability and authorization are process-, product-, market- and period-specific.**
3. **Buyer behaviour is part of supply-chain risk.** Syntha measures purchasing practices as well as supplier performance.
4. **Assessment completion is not remediation.** Outcomes and effectiveness must be tracked separately.
5. **Worker grievance data is highly restricted and purpose-bound.**
6. **External facility identifiers improve interoperability but do not replace canonical identity stewardship.**
7. **Capacity recovery cannot assume hidden overtime or undeclared subcontracting.**
8. **Supplier exit does not terminate unresolved remedy obligations.**
